# The Hidden Cost of Cheap IT: What a Day of Downtime Costs a Santa Fe Business

A simple guide and calculator from **ZIA Networks** to help small business
owners work out what one bad IT day really costs.

> The $75-a-month plan looks great until the Tuesday your whole office goes dark.

## Why this exists

Most owners treat IT as a monthly expense to keep as low as possible. But IT
isn't really a cost. It's the floor everything else stands on. When it cracks,
you don't lose "tech." You lose the whole day.

## Quick example

These numbers are illustrative, but realistic for a small Santa Fe business.
Replace them with your own.

A 10-person office earning about $1.8M a year, over roughly 250 working days,
makes about **$7,200 on a normal day**.

| Cost of one full day offline      | Estimate            |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------|
| Idle payroll (10 people, 8 hrs)   | $3,200              |
| Lost or delayed revenue           | $3,600              |
| Emergency IT help                 | $1,000 - $1,500     |
| Catch-up work and overtime        | ~$800               |
| **Total**                         | **~$9,000 - $9,500**|

And that's only the part you can count.

## Costs that don't fit in a spreadsheet

- **Customers who quietly leave.** They rarely complain. They just stop calling.
- **Team stress and morale.** Good employees remember days like that.
- **Data you can't get back.** A backup that has never been tested isn't a
  backup. It's a hope.
- **Compliance trouble** if you handle client, health, or financial records.

## Santa Fe has its own version of this problem

- Older adobe and historic buildings with wiring never planned for modern offices
- Monsoon season power flickers and lightning strikes
- Patchy internet outside the main corridors
- A small market, so there's often no backup if your only IT person is away
- Tourism-driven businesses can't make up a lost peak-season Saturday

## What "cheap IT" often leaves out

- [ ] Active monitoring, so problems are caught before your staff notice
- [ ] Backups that are regularly test-restored
- [ ] Real response times, not "within 48 hours"
- [ ] Documentation of your setup
- [ ] Regular updates and security patches

## Use the calculator

```bash
python downtime_cost.py
```

Answer the prompts and you'll get your own estimate.

## Five questions to ask this week

1. If our internet or main system went down at 9 a.m., who would I call, and
   how fast would they respond?
2. When was our backup last test-restored?
3. Do I know what a full day of downtime costs us?
4. Is our setup written down?
5. Is anyone watching for problems before we notice them?

If you hesitated on more than two, it's worth a conversation.
